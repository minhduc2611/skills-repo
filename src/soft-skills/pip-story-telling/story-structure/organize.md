# Story Bank (Collect your best stories)

Keep track of your stories—and the information behind them—if you don’t want to lose our trust.

Stories make you the centre of attention. But boring people or breaking confidence loses trust. Create a Story Bank: a collection of data and metadata (information about the stories).

Tip: put the Story Bank in a collaborative online space for team sharing.

We mess up when we tell stories that are:
- Boring: telling the same story twice to the same person.
- Disrespectful: wasting the audience’s time.
- Gossipy: repeat a story we were told in confidence.
- Stolen: pass someone else’s story off as our own.
- Stale: a story that’s past its sell-by date.

You need a Story Bank to avoid these pitfalls. Use a notebook or spreadsheet with these headings:

| Heading | Question |
|---|---|
| **What’s the story?** | Keywords are usually all you need. |
| **Why would I tell this story?** | What’s the point? |
| **Who might find this story useful?** | Audience / use case. |
| **Who have I told this story to already?** | Avoid repeats. |
| **Whose story is it?** | Is it OK for me to share it? |
| **Is this story still up to date?** | Currency / relevance. |

An online Story Bank also lets you link to posts, articles, and shared documents.


# Big, Small, Inside, Outside (Tell the right story)

You might have a Big Story to tell the world. But you’ll spend more time telling small stories to colleagues.

- Big stories: rehearsed explanations of “why we do this” or “what’s coming next,” usually delivered one-to-many.
- Small stories: spontaneous examples of “how we do things round here,” usually told in conversation.
- Inside: you tell to people you work with.
- Outside: you tell to everyone else.

Tip: try this alongside [Organise] Audience Profile to start planning your stories.

| | **Inside** | **Outside** |
|---|---|---|
| **Big Stories** | Induction, Awards | Pitch, Recruitment, Conference |
| **Small Stories** | Appraisal, Training, Culture, Habit | Blog, Brand, Media, Social |
