# Recipe

## Stories that Sell (show your value)

Convince people that you can deliver. Stories work better than spreadsheets when it comes to building trust.
Selling is about so much more than price, quality or your
"value proposition". You can't sell without trust. Can we trust you to understand what we need? Can we trust you to deliver on the promise you're making about an uncertain future? Selling is a complex human process.
Stories help you reach people and win them over.

1. [Organize] Audience Profile
Who is your audience? What kind of problems are you solving for them?
2. [Function] Simple Sales Stories
Show how you've helped someone they can relate to.
3. [Explore] Social Proof
Look for trends, prototypes or testimonials that will strengthen your case.
4. [Structure] Rags to Riches
Try an optimistic story structure, with your customer at its heart.
5. [Function] Pitch Perfect
Boil it down to an elevator pitch.


## Stories that Connect (Get closer to people)

Walk a mile in another person's shoes. Stories are a great way to see things from a different point of view.
How can you make the right product or service for someone if you don't really know them? How can you lead a team if you don't listen to your colleagues?
Stories can plug us into other people's thoughts, feelings and experiences. Stories build empathy, and empathy helps you build better ideas.

1. [Explore] Story Listening
Reach out to people by listening to the wisdom contained in their stories.
2. [Explore] Abstractions
Don't just ask questions. Watch how people behave too.

3. [Concept] Universal Stories
Look for common ground and shared experiences.

4. [Function] Story-ish Conversations
Start looking for stories in everyday situations.

5. [Concept] Circle of Life
Develop relatable stories based on characters and journeys we all recognise.

## Stories that Impress (Nail that presentation!)

Present your ideas with confidence. Don't bury them under dusty layers of business jargon.
Hook our attention, make your facts flow like a story and master the art of show and tell. You've got an important message: say it with style!

1. [Style] Movie Time
Make sure you're actually telling a story, not just giving facts and opinions

2. [Structure] A Five Ts
Get the beats of your story right with a simple structure.

3. [Style] Story Hooks
Grab your audience's attention from start to finish.

4. [Style] Show and Tell
Don't let your visual presentation bore or confuse people.

5. [Function] Cut to the Chase
Have a plan in case your presentation falls


## Stories that Motivate (Make people act)

Get people behind you. Persuade us to take a leap of faith and support your idea.
When you want things to change, you'll need to show us a plan: the details of your idea. But that's not enough. We also want to know what's driving you.
After all, you're asking us to trust you, to follow you into the unknown.

1. [Concept] The Dragon & the City
What are you trying to achieve? Big picture stuff.

2. [Character] Drive Stories
What's motivating you? What might motivate us too?

3. [Concept] Three Great Conflicts
What's stopping you? What barriers must you overcome?

4. [Structure] A Innovation Curve
Reassure us about the risks you're asking us to take.

5. [Structure] A No Easy Way
Give us a realistic idea of the journey ahead.


## Stories that Explain (Clarify your goals)

Bring your strategy to life. Show why those abstract statements matter in the real world.
How many people understand the strategic direction of their organisation? How many care? Most strategy documents sit on the digital equivalent of a dusty shelf.
But your strategy is actually an attempt to bring clarity to chaos, to choose your path through a changing world. That sounds like a great story, waiting to be told.

1. [Concept] Order & Chaos
Where do you stand in an ever-changing world?

2. [Concept] Good & Evil
Which side do you take in the important battles?

3. [Character] What's it About?
Why does this strategy matter to your colleagues?

4. [Style] Rolls Royce Moment
Help us see your strategy in action.

5. [Style] Story Hooks
Make your strategy sound interesting enough to actually read.


## Stories that Lead (Show the way)

Build a stronger team by learning from each other’s stories of struggles, triumphs and setbacks.
Does everyone in your team understand the rules of 'how we do things round here'? Does everyone appreciate the experience and wisdom among their colleagues?
Your team culture is too important to leave to chance. Make your team more cohesive and inclusive by sharing stories of what 'good' looks like.

1. [Explore] Curious Tales
Find out what really makes your team members tick.

2. [Structure] Man in a Hole
Frame the work you’re doing together as an epic journey.

3. [Explore] Emotional Dashboard
Find stories in the highs and lows of any project.

4. [Explore] Thoughtful Failures
Extract the wisdom of mistakes and setbacks after a project.

5. [Organise] Story Bank
Collect useful stories all the team can learn from.


## Stories that Convince (Explain your expertise)

Explain years of experience and hours of research to a non-expert audience. Get them to back your judgement.
It’s annoying: you can have all the facts at your fingertips, but still people won’t listen. Maybe it’s your fault. Are you overloading your audience with data? Have you turned an exciting process of discovery into a dull spreadsheet? Do we know enough about you to trust your judgement?

1. [Style] Three is the Magic Number
Put your audience first. How many facts can you expect them to remember?

2. [Explore] That’s Funny
Share the excitement behind your own insights.

3. [Explore] Data Detectives
Handle your facts in a story-ish way.

4. [Character] Trust Me, I’m an Expert
Show us why we should trust you.

5. [Character] Hero & Guide
Put yourself in the story as the Expert Guide, helping us on our way.
